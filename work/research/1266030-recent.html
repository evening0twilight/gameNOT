TLDR: Not worth it for 20 quid, barely worth it for 5. Too much wrong to explain why, too much good enough to say don’t.

General Thoughts/Overview:
The Pale Beyond (PB) is a difficult one to pin down. A resource management and decision simulator, PB carefully skips along the line between being a game and being a glorified visual novel. Too easy to beat on normal, but also too hard to perfect in one go… The whole purpose of this story seems to be to explore different options, but the game actively works against you in doing exactly that with some absolutely baffling and terrible game design choices.

This is 100% one of those games where Steam needs a neutral rating, because as the kids would say this is ‘mid’.

So what do we do? Well, we’re the first mate of the good ship Temperance on a rescue mission into the Antarctic, and we’re going to have a bad time on this odyssey as everything goes wrong. We balance food and fuel with morale and health, and the needs of the various crew members, as we try to survive. Basically we send people out to complete jobs for resources, then spend those resources on surviving.

So pull your boots on and let’s head into the storm.

Pros:
  • An icy survival adventure set in the age of exploration?! Oh my, yes please! This is such an underappreciated and underused genre, and to have a game set in it is glorious.
  • The overarching story and narrative is interesting enough. It kept me engaged and I did wonder the whole way through what I was going to find come the end, and if anyone would survive.
  • The characters are fairly well thought through, and several of the main ones do have arc’s you can see to completion… although the people I found most interesting were missed off.

Cons:
  • No skip cutscene button. Christ. This is a game BUILT around going back and changing your choices to try and branch off in a new direction, which weapons grade buffoon left a flipping skip cutscene button out?! Maybe the first play through, sure, but once you’re awakened? I cannot be dealing with this.
  • PB suffers the standard problem of written narrative choose your own adventure dialogue in that you have NO idea the tone a statement is going to be delivered in, thus no idea of the impact of said tone. Something which reads to you as a joke is clearly delivered and taken in all seriousness by the characters, making some portions of the more complex and interesting dialogue a nightmare to traverse, not because the writing is bad, but because tone is not implicit.
  • Speaking of, the writing is pretty bad at points. I’m looking at you week 37. There were so many ways this could have been done better, so many missed opportunities for this to blossom into an incredibly poignant and memorable moment. And instead? We got tone shifting, illogical, and above all just pointless drama from temu.
  • The UI is borked and has just never been fixed. From menu’s breaking, to text not appearing, to the mouse not registering, to the sliders not moving, to click boxes being outside the circles… The game occasionally needed restarting just to get the menus going again. This is mixed in with an unclear inventory system and a lot of bits that don’t make sense when thought about too much.

Suggested improvements:
  • First of all the game needs finishing. It seems to me to be blindingly clear that backgrounds should have had a much bigger role both mechanically and with relationships/dialogue options. Hell, the vast majority of the game seems to be fluff, it needs more and better paths to follow based on who lives, and who is loyal.
  • Second, FIX THE DAMN UI. And add a skip cutscene button while you’re at it.
  • Finally, please clean up the systems and make them clearer to use.


Overall Recommendation:
PB is so hard to give an overall rating for. On the one hand, I feel like a negative review would bring more attention to the massive issues the game has. But then again, does it deserve a negative review? It’s not a bad game per se, just extremely flawed. I want to see more form this dev, and if they can improve on their formula then everyone wins. I think the biggest issue is that this is a 5 quid game quality with a 20 quid price tag…

So who is it for? I recommend this to people who like choose your own adventures, I recommend this to people who like survival stories, and I recommend this to people who like narrative driven games.

I don’t recommend this to anyone who spams spacebar, I don’t recommend this to people looking for a good challenge, and I don’t recommend this to people looking for fastpaced action packed whizz-bang.

I guess I have to give this a thumbs up, because I want more of it, just better done.
