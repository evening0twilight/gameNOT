This is not really for fans of Slay the Spire. The card aspect is basic and it doesn't ask you to do much.

But it's a beautiful video game story, and it destroyed me. I cried a lot. Actions really do have consequences, the characters feel 3D and real, and the tone feels real, not saccharine. Also, i appreciated how queer and poly it was, and I liked Marz's side story about inequality, and themes of resisting fascism, of the importance of caring about those different from you...but it's done in like...it's not preachy, it feels very natural and mostly given the proper amount of weight.

I do have some quibbles. Toppling right-wing governments isn't really that simple, there should have been more resistance even after you win. Cal really gets into your face about pacifism. The way to have Tammy choose you over Cal is pretty ♥♥♥♥♥♥ up. You cannot actually kill a few people who would probably be better off dead.

And I do wish there was more polyamory, and I do feel feel sad that a girl I really liked was aro. But I guess that's just how life is.

But these are very small. I wish everyone would play this game. It feels so real. There are tragic moments that make your successes feel triumphant. There are a lot of awww-inducing childhood scenes, the writers are damn good at capturing well a childlike voice, where things that are small to grown-ups feel significant and wonderful.

And I hope everyone who liked IWATE checks out ExtraColonist, it's a super good fan expansion and they deserve so much love.

I don't write a lot of reviews, but this was genuinely one of the most heartwarming and comfy games I've ever had the pleasure to come accross. You can tell how much love and attention the developers poured into the game with the dialogue and the writing, as well as with the countless endings you can get based off of every minute decisions you make in-game. It really does feel like you (the protagonist) and your teenage friend group grew up together in this fantastically written coming-of-age story as you explore different themes such as cohabilitation, freedom, rebellion, romance and more. Plus I'm also a sucker for when you grind a bit in a video game and the stats go up, and this game just so happens to provide that for me as well. Highly recommend if you're experiencing burnout with life or with playing video games, and want to play something that's equally refreshing and comforting.

This is an extrodinarily beautiful game, with great immesurable writing, and delivering a unique experience you'll never have experienced before - the setting, card-minigame, and characters are all very fun to meet and play through the stories off

Where this charm fall short is the unceremonious endings...
Through every playthrough and ending I've experienced playing this game - more often than not - they've ended rather depressingly. Either unsatisfying or unexpected, a short description of an epiloque will play at the end of a 10 hour playthrough describing a dreery ending...

||Your romantic partner dies suddenly, or dump you for someone else... your friends live unsatisfying lives and you grow old before dying: summarises nearly every epiloque I've read. Even after acheiving the 'good' ending this dialogue plays before the game ends.||

The game will then encourage you to play again to achieve something happier - then beginning the core gameplay loop of reliving your life knowing how to make things better - yet despite that, it still delivers a rather diminishing ending...

I honestly loved the journey the game put me on, with all the interactions and character throughout it, as I got very attached to the story, characters and narrative. Though dissapointed by the ending, I held out hope I could make it better; yet despite that the game's writing likes to be cataclismic and dramatic... which gets sour after awhile

I reccomend playing this game, especially if you enjoy a sci-fi setting, but I hope this review can serve as a content warning, since the story can be quite depressing
