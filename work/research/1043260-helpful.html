Sometimes you buy a game because you’re in the mood to manage a team of gladiators.
And sometimes you buy it because you’re avoiding doing your laundry.

Either way, Gladiator Guild Manager is a fun little game.

The concept is easy to understand — you build a team of fighters, you make them punch and zap each other in small arenas, and then you do it again, but with slightly fancier boots.

The campaign gives you a nice reason to keep playing. The endless mode is there if you want to feel like you’re doing something impressive, even though it’s mostly just numbers going up.

There was a bug after an update that deleted my entire roster…
except for two guys who became completely unstoppable.
Kind of like when my boss downsized my department, replaced everyone with much more efficient, unstoppable AI. Now I have more time to play GGM I guess.

Anyway, some classes are clearly better than others. But there’s a nice sense of rock-paper-scissors balance, like schoolyard arguments or deciding what to wear outside in autumn. I chose rain coat but I draw warm sunshine again wtf

This isn’t the best game you’ll ever buy.
But it doesn’t need to be.

It’s the kind of game you pick up for a few quid, play for a weekend, and then remember two months later like an oddly specific dream.
You won’t tell your friends it changed your life.
But you might smile when you see it in your Steam library and think, “Oh yeah… you.”

Pretty fun out of 10.

What appears on the surface to be some kinda crappy ported mobile game, with its 'blue bullcrap' diamond MTX resource being converted to be grind-able, quickly proves itself to be anything but.

It's a very well put together little auto battler with tons of items and builds to grind out and dozens of units to unlock. It also has the ability to rename units which is great for immersion and fun. Some of the units have some pathing issues, but these haven't been a stick in the wheel for me.

The unit variety is great, with everyone filling a niche. You have everything from your traditional knights wizards and healers, to a hobo with a strength scaling AOE fart cloud attack... And there is an item that causes AOE abilities to set enemies on fire... I don't think there is a game other than South Park that allows you to build an explosive / flaming farts playstyle.

I can imagine this game is tremendously difficult to balance, especially for new players, so give the guy time with this, he has his feedback. The difficulty spikes, even on normal difficulty are something to behold. If you like that sort of thing it is a plus, but if you like a constant stream of progression it is not.

I will note, that I've noticed similar spikes in power on some of the characters I've been experimenting with and building, due to some interesting stat scaling items.

For example, while building a metal executioner, I found some items that give massive % to strength but at certain costs like reducing his movement speed. The result is a character that can hit for 13000 with his execution attack despite only being level 15. This chunks the HP bar of even boss characters that you're supposed to grind against for a bit.

The campaign is also well made, with you getting access to different things from different factions depending on your relations with them. I've played through a couple of times now and been offered access to different units. This run, the baroness has offered me some soldiers, and I don't remember this happening before.

I recommend this game, and the current price is just right for a game like this - it's still just an auto battler, keep in mind. Just a very good one.

Thank you for reading my review.

ABANDONWARE - CAUTION

The core loop looks appealing at first glance. Then the design collapses. Too many classes. No balance. Several units are built for AI only: suicidal, expendable, either overtuned or pure annoyance.

Not talking about bosses or quest minions.

Necromancers: a unit useful only after something is already dead. Works for AI. Fails for player.

Roughly 30% of melee classes are dead weight. Stun orcs: too weak to engage, too slow to reach archers, too low damage to kill DD. To make them function you must invest in speed, HP, armor, damage, agility. Result: expensive bullet fodder.

Viable builds are limited to single-stat abuse: archers, taunt-spiked tanks, warlocks/banshees. Everything else is strictly inferior.

Invisible melee storm chasers: first hit deals extreme damage with AOE stun. Cannot be manually targeted because invisible. If positioning fails, you lose a unit before interaction. After that, the attacker becomes useless. Building this unit as player is pointless: AI outnumbers you, 1-for-1 trades are losing trades.

Even bad balance could be tolerated. The real problem: critical bugs.

* Abilities frequently not working
* Random crashes
* Inconsistent damage
* Bugged deaths

Check Steam discussions: 100+ reports. No dev response.

Devs publicly stated they are done with the game and moved to another project.

Advice: skip the game. Ignore the developer.

I was so disappointed in the this game for the fact it is so heavily time gated.
