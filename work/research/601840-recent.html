The way I would describe this is "surprisingly good". I'm not sure what it is, maybe the art style, maybe the two-deck system clearly focusing on dialogue and story way more than the average game in this genre (since I am someone who usually skips almost all dialogue and story), but for whatever reason, after getting this on a deep sale, I just sort of avoided it, played other stuff instead. I was wrong! This game is really good, and now that I finally got into it, I'm hooked!

I'll cut to the chase: yes, the way this game is designed likely will kill the sort of "infinite" replayability that you get with a really good roguelike, such as Monster Train 1 and 2. However, there's a very intelligent trade-off here: until you exhaust the options, each replay will actually have quite a bit more to interest the player than the average "ok, one more" in other roguelikes. The reasoning here is the story: since there's so much focus on story, I'm sure it will eventually get stale to repeat the same events/days, since there's only so many options (basically talk or fight, with some extra variety in many situations as well)... however, right now, only 5hrs in, I've only played the 2nd and 3rd character once, and the initial character twice, and I still think there's at least one or two things I could do differently as her on a third playthrough. Simple math says I'll be playing for at least another 10 hours, maybe 15+, and that's more than enough for the price even if I never come back after that! Plus, like I said, each new game as a specific character isn't just "ok, off to fight the random mobs and bosses again!", it's "how am I going to play their story this time?" which is very cool and unique in the genre.

For example, the first time as the third character, for somewhat spoiler-y reasons I won't say, I decided that he was insanely selfish, beyond a fault, and therefore used murder to solve almost every situation. I'm pretty excited to go back and recharacterize him as a f*** up who is trying his best to be better, to climb up out of his self-imposed gutter and make a name for himself in this city. This will force me to make all kinds of different choices and let me see different scenarios and dialogue (plus, play the whole game differently as this will require more of a focus on the negotiation deck).

Even as someone who skips a lot of story, and I do go QUITE quickly through a lot of the dialogue, I'm actually pretty interested in the storylines here (at least in broadstrokes, I haven't seen any bad writing but I also haven't found most of the simple conversations to be life-changing or whatever, not that I would expect that). I even sometimes engage in the uncessary dialogue, the options that clearly aren't giving me hints or useful items, just to see what people say or how they act towards me. That's gotta be at least good writing since I usually couldn't care less.

Anyway, oh yeah, the game, right... the game is pretty good too! I'm not sure how I feel about each character, so far it seems clear that the first one is way better at negotiation than combat, and the second is the reverse, so this does push the player towards one playstyle over the other but maybe I'm just not locking in the right strategies or getting the right random drops for new cards. In any case, I'm actually pretty impressed with some elements of the gameplay too, for example I like how, while you can find card upgrades as rewards, all cards can automatically be upgraded at the end of battle once you've used them enough. I was also getting worried that there wasn't any meta-progression, but now I have new outfits and a special store specifically for meta-upgrades, and a perk system... To be honest I can't really think of anything this game does "wrong" except that the art style isn't my favourite (for people, it works quite well for aliens and robots!). I guess it can be hard to get money / really make a specific build? But this game isn't really about that, it's about following a story and making choices along the way. I guess my only real critique is I've played for 5hrs and I still have almost no idea what is happening during the negotiations. Sometimes I add stuff to their orbit, they add stuff to my orbit, it feels like if I just do "damage" a lot and put up my shields a lot, I win... so I've been winning a lot so I must understand it, right? Except I don't, a lot of the time I either take or do damage I wasn't expecting, and I did lose once because of that, so I guess that part of the game could be less complex and/or more clearly explained and/or have better signposting and tooltips. Still, it's been fun to learn each of the three styles, for both negotiation and combat, and I'm having enough fun to easily excuse one myseterious loss.

All in all, if you can go into this with the right mindset, and generally like deckbuilders, I think you'll have a blast (that mindset being: this is not a wall of floors and random bosses to hit your head against over and over until you finally win, this is a choose your own adventure book with a very good, or at least decent, card game that functions as the glue between events and the reason for things happening or not happening). Especially if you see a sale, buy it! And to be honest, if I even play for 10hrs, I've paid more money than the full price here for less fun than that before. Give it a chance! So far it doesn't even have any like IdPol bs either despite the focus on the female character in the art, it's just good ol' fashioned steampunk noir sci-fi style goodness!

This game seriously needs its flowers, I have never played anything more addicting and enthralling in my life. Everything from the setting, to the mechanics, to the art style, and to the music all coalesce to form something truly unique.
I haven't played many deck builders, so some elements may not seem so innovative to me as they are, but by god it feels like there's so much thought put into the inner workings.
I feel like this is properly seen if you try to engage with its achievements, the game encourages for you to find its exploits and discover all of these interesting synergies that you'd otherwise never think of without them in mind.
Each character on their own boast a wide variety of tactics at their disposal and it never feels like the game cheats you out of victory if you have a well-polished deck.
The storymode, brawl, and daily challenges are such fun ways to interact with all the niches and that's further expanded upon with all of the cool workshop content I've experienced.
It doesn't feel like this game receives much attention by the wider gaming community, but the love is here all the same and I'm glad to be a part of it.
