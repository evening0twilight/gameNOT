Incredible First Impression, Fumbles Badly at the End
The first four hours of this game are incredible, with great art, writing, music, and an engaging twist on deckbuilder gameplay that isn't too difficult but also encourages you to play optimally. The story is compelling and surprisingly dark; it takes some big swings and doesn't shy away from the consequences of living in a new colony on an alien planet.

All of that changes after about the 2/3rds point, though. The random events start to repeat, the overall plot becomes strained and disjointed, huge world-altering character storylines are introduced and resolved in a single short story segment, the card game becomes trivially easy even on hard mode, the list goes on. There are three issues that stand out to me as particularly egregious:

  • It takes so long to actually enter into a romantic relationship with any of the characters that, by the time you finally do, all of the activities you can do that give statistical rewards and consistently result in interactions with them are useless. And even if you do do an activity for no mechanical progress, very few if any of the events change based on your romantic status (or if they did, it was impossible for me to tell that they did). You can explicitly interact romantically with your partner, but there were only two scenes for who I ended up with-- one of them repeated five times.
  • By a similar token, the cracks in the activity system really begin to show in the last few years. I maxed out the activities in the engineering lab and did a smattering of other activities, but when I went to do other activities the writing would be... off. Either the events would have the adults communicating with me as if I was a child, or they assumed familiarity with character interactions that I just never saw, etc.
  • The ending was incredibly unsatisfying. There was obvious social and political upheaval imminent, the yearly alien attacks hadn't been resolved, we hadn't secured a stable food source, etc. But instead of addressing any of those issues I just got a specific epilogue for my romantic interest (wherein we broke up after a couple years, then they worked themselves to death...) and a generic "and everyone else lived happily ever after" and an invitation to play through it all again.

There's of course tons of content I didn't see, and I can see how some of the things I didn't explore could address some of these issues-- but based on discussions I've seen and how fundamental some of these flaws were I have no desire to play through this game again (let alone 28 more times to get all of the endings, if this ending was any indication of the general quality of them). But despite all that I can't stop thinking about this game-- the characters, the world, and the art are so compelling and it makes me so sad that the ending soured me so much on the experience.

Mechanically wonderful, the story is engaging, the endings make my heart ache (not in the best way)

Without spoilers:

I had been eyeing this game for a while, and the first few hours were a blast!

*The card game aspect is good, it's challenging when it needs to be and not annoying the rest of the time, fairly balanced and the art for the cards is great.
*The story took me by surprise, especially during my first run. The replayability aspect is there if your focus is the story, and certain important events are made easier once you "solve" them for the first time (which makes repeating loops way less tedious, I'm thankful for that!)
*The stats you need for certain choices never felt wrongly placed imo, I encountered some events where I couldn't do anything too special without certain stats but it doesn't significantly hurt your progress. If you're able to raise your stats and pick those options you do get rewarded, so that part feels fair as well.

MY ONLY ISSUE: The story doesn't wrap up as nicely as I'd expect, given the work you put in for certain endings. It feels as though I've given it my all, and still some things just happen that leave me unsatisfied.

**heavily censored spoilers here**
There's what I assume would be the "best possible/ good" ending where you negotiate peace with the aliens which took me some effort, certainly more than my first 2 endings, so I expected it to make life in the colony all better. Which, it did, with the exception of the romantic aspect. The goal of the game is to explore your choices, and try to live different lives to then achieve a better result than last time.

In my case, I wanted to go for Dys as my partner. And HOLY COW, I FEEL...lied to??? I felt as though the story wanted to convince me to push past his negativity because we were both the outcasts and supported each other. And then put effort into resolving the game's conflict at the same time, to have a happy resolution as a reward for my efforts and planning.

In the end, no matter what I do, no matter how hard I try to cater to either him, the colony, or the aliens , or all at the same time, the relationship just ends up crumbling when he inevitably, in every ending related to him, leaves. And this isn't even just Dys, relationships with other characters have equally disappointing things happen down the line.

TL;DR / closing thoughts

I like the themes of the story, how it makes you put in effort for the rewards, and how some conflicts are just meant to happen while you can change other outcomes. If you want to focus on the story of the colony and help it thrive (or not) the game is wonderful and I 100% recommend it. BUT if you want to ALSO enjoy a romantic story where the ending feels rewarding for your time and effort, this is not the game for that.

Posted: 1 July, 2025
!! Spoilers for some early events, how the player can affect them, and discussions of the game's romances !!

This game has a lot of moving parts. It's ambitious, you can see that from word go, and it is, overall, competently executed. I love the visuals, the character designs are distinct, I have this weird fixation with min/maxing in life sims so I can see myself coming back periodically just to see how high I can get my stats by the end of the game. It's a good way to pass time, the sole minigame's simple but satisfying(it does start to feel stale after a while, but I took that as my sign to take a break).

But I felt like I saw a lot of cracks after a few hours. The story and world don't feel meaningfully reactive, it feels like it's running a bunch of binary yes/no checks.

For example, in my first playthrough I chose Tammy as my character's childhood best friend. MC's mother and Tammy's father both died, they had a shared history, high affinity, and MC found her crying due to grief. MC cried with her, but the fact they both lost a parent within, like, a year wasn't acknowledged. I'm not saying the game should have pivoted the scene to focus on MC's feelings instead of Tammy's, but it felt jarring to be talking about grief without mentioning that. I expected some level of commiseration, either MC saying "hey, I get it, I just lost a parent too" or, given Tammy's personality, that she would've asked how MC was feeling and tried to shift the conversation to *their* grief. But that would take time to write and program, so instead the game checks if Tonin is alive then runs one of two conversations.

It just makes the world feel flat, and, unfortunately, the game's full of moments like that. You pick MC's childhood best friend, but that doesn't affect anything outside of how many affinity points they start with. It sounds like a goofy thing to complain about, but since you start off with a cast of 7 kids, you'd think they'd chat about that kind of thing, especially as they become teens and start to drift apart(I could see a lot of angsty "we never hang out anymore" conversations, particularly when dating comes into the mix).

You also choose an augment for MC, things like unnaturally good eyesight or an extremely calm temperament or extra fingers. Other characters have augments, too, and they're huge parts of their personalities. But not MC's, their augment adds a card to the minigame deck and increases a stat at the start, that's basically it. I didn't see it mentioned by other characters at all during dialogue or flavor text. So, sure, the game lets you choose from a bunch of options, but it rarely feels like it matters. Save Tonin and he immediately retires, you never see him again. Save Eudicot, she acquieses to Lum and effectively bows out of the story. Save MC's mom and her sprite stands near their dad's on the overworld.

I would have preferred a few options that actually have an effect on things instead of a bunch that don't. Maybe choose one character to be MC's best friend and just let the player define what that means, how their relationship formed and developed early on. Nem would've been a great choice, she could've been the outgoing extrovert who pulls a shy MC out of their shell or raised hell with a more mischevieous MC, icing them out regardless once she starts grieving. It also would've given the player a reason to side with Helios outside of "this is a game, nothing matters and I want to choose the cartoonishly authoritarian option". And it could've added more weight to the opening cutscene once it loops back around. Give players a set parameter(their best friend, their special quirk) and let them define what that means to them(whether it draws them out of their shell or reinforces confidence, becomes a crutch or is written off).

As it stands, MC just doesn't feel like a part of the gameworld. They desperately need a more reactive element to ground them in it. Relationships, platonic and romantic, suffer the most there. I never believed any of these characters were into MC except Rex, and MC/character friendships are never acknowledged outside of affinity checks. MC always feels like a second choice for romances(again, except with Rex) regardless of their affinity with any given character. Cal and Tammy are obsessed with each other and difficult to split up. Marz and Tang aren't actually interested in relationships, although MC can still pursue them. Dys will always have a crush on an alien. Rex and Nomi can form a cute triad with MC, romancing Rex alone results in some weird moments with Nomi but is overall sweet. If you want a monogamous relationship, it's gonna be a lot of work and a little luck, plus it very well might end abruptly and unsatisfactorily. I understand relationships don't always work out IRL, people aren't always compatible, I'm totally cool with MC getting shot down by a character(Serafen my beloved...) but it ultimately just feels like MC doesn't need to be there.

MC/character relationships also aren't acknowledged unless you fought to break up a couple. MC's parents don't have any thoughts on who their only child's dating, Marz' dads don't have a moment with MC if they pursue her. In general, characters outside the main cast don't feel like part of the world. They're mentioned in passing, you almost never see them, the overworld's empty except for romanceables and MC's parent(s). Having minor characters randomly crop up and give us short scenes to help flesh out the colony would've been nice. Instead, they're just numbers. 100 people live here(you will see a handful of them), 17 people died, etc.

MC really feels like they exist in a vacuum. Yes, because they're the special-est kid in the world, they can change the outcome of the story, they can choose who lives or dies, but nobody really seems attached to them and whether or not someone survives barely matters.

The epilogue's brief. It doesn't talk about anyone outside of MC's immediate circle, just their partner(s) and parent(s). It would've been nice to get a line or two about what other characters were doing. I'm not saying every character needs a post-ending novella, a simple "And Cal, who you hung out with a couple times, took over Geoponics eventually. He's happy/sad about life in the colony" would've been enough for me.

On a game that sells itself, partially, on replayability, a shallow relationship system and unsatisfying endings make it kind of hard for me to recommend. Like I said, it's pretty and I could see myself checking the "skip seen dialogue" box, turning on a podcast, and trying to speedrun it for various endings, but I was ultimately disappointed in the game overall. It had its moments, it's not like it was unplayable or I'm kicking myself for spending money on it, I just thought the experience as a whole lacked the depth a project like this needs to shine.

EDIT:

After reading a few other reviews I feel I should mention that the progressivism is ultimately a thin veneer. It posits living in a post-racial society where humanity's past mistakes are hammered into kids' heads(good! I like that) but concepts like white supremacy or the realities of assimilation never come up. We never really get to hear an argument against cultural assimilation. Beyond that, the only black character you're likely to see is a fierce independent girl who sees everyone as below her, and there's a hyperintelligent stoic East Asian girl with a fiery temper. The text itself furthers racist stereotypes, regardless of the writers intentions. Combined with their unwillingness to acknowledge real-world racism in the text, some design and writing choices feel disingenuous if not slightly insidious.

This game is a very interesting case. It fails utterly as a game that tells a compelling, responsive story; but it is incredibly successful as a kind of an incremental game where you see numbers go up and ‘collect’ valuable resources—endings—through those numbers. Unfortunately, that is not the type of game that I was seeking for.

Initially, I was enamoured with the game and its premise, and the first loss that most of players experience hit me like a truck—to an extent that my partner came in to ask me why I was crying in the middle of the day. As I progressed through the story, though, I realised that every other loss (and major plot point) is basically dictated by very arbitrary factors that are meant to be accounted for in your future playthroughs, and not by decisions that make sense for your given character. That makes sense in a way that you play as a character that is meant not to dwell on any particular ‘life’ but instead engage them on a meta-level, but practically that just means that you are effectively detached from any events that happen in any given life; any hardships ring hollow and ultimately do not matter. All of that creates an incredibly ‘gamey’ feel to the way you interact with the game, turning every character into just a bunch of numbers—and a stepping stone towards a desired outcome. It’s an interesting peek into the way an immortal entity might have interacted with the world, but ultimately it’s an incredibly cold, calculated and pointless experience that is, to my mind, at odds with the vulnerable, earnest and caring promise of the beginning of the game.

The thing that corroded the experience even further is that the story (probably because of the focus on the meta-progress) is written in a very limited way, that is quickly revealed if you zero-in on a particular archetype, making it obvious that the game has a very particular idea of what should happen—and is very blind to things that fall outside of that, to an extent that borders on ridiculous.
For instance, one of my playthroughs was with an incredibly combat-oriented character (Physical stats maxed out, consistently out to protect the colony in the Glow, a sworn-in member of the garrison and ultimately the second in command). Yet, right till the end of the game, every time I chose to fight in the Glow, the game treated it as a surprise (“what? you want to defend the walls? well, you can try…”) despite this being my literal job in the colony at this point—so the game simply could not account for a very basic choice, making the holes in the story ever more visible.
Another fun example is the event in the Swamps where you run into a vat-grown Faceless with Vance. Despite the fact that I effectively defeated three fully grown Faceless monsters in the past, one of them on my lonesome own, and also beat Vance in every possible contest at this point, the game completely disregarded all of that lived-in story to describe our characters to be “frozen in fear” (at the weakened inactive juvenile Faceless, mind you) and offered my battle-hardened shotgun-on-legs of a girl only two options: grab Vance and run away… or plead with Vance out of all people so that he could kindly allow me to fight on his side (which I hilariously couldn’t even do because my Persuasion wasn’t high enough—specifically because I went in on Physical stats). I burst out laughing at this point.
Combine this story blindness/linearity with the focus on the meta-layer, and you get “I Was a Teenage Exocolonist.”

I really really really wanted to like this game. Even after becoming disillusioned with the way it works, I pushed as far as I could to give it a fair assessment. Ultimately, though, this is not a game that I can recommend.

9 hours in and I just can't take it anymore. Maybe I'll come back to this game to finish my run, but I feel like I'm playing an Idle game that doesn't realize it's an Idle game.
