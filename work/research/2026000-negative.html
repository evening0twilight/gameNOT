Ok, so I've realized what my issue is with this game. The game in inherently fun, I played it all the way until the end. If you like these types of games, don't read the rest, play it. Why the bad review then? Because I think this game needs another year of bake time before it's truly good, and the fact there's a good game under here just makes each problem irk me more.
-A lack of party dialogues (big miss here, the game has good dialogue in the actual story bits, so why not more to show as the parties grow?)
-only 5 story adventurers even though you have 6 party slots. also the last one just doesn't seem to get a story quest?
-The fact there's very little benefit for not only have one party (arguably detrimental by the fact master classes and titles are unique and the story characters you get are just WAY better than everyone else)
-Stat growths are silly and dumb (more of a personal opinion)
-Only 6 master classes? Really? Especially the fact there's just not one for tanks
-Without AP modifiers you only get a single action in combat, making it super simple early
-Hiding mechanics are absolutely broken early and generally useless later on when more and more enemies get permanent true sight
-HP scalings are whack because only melee classes get endurance so your squishies will get oneshot late game.
-Rogues are useless early compared to other damage dealers, but lategame have so many crit and sneak attack modifiers they become like 3 damage dealers in one.
-Bravery points mean boss fights last one turn once you reach the boss because you get 3 turns of attack in one (see only one action per turn normally to see why this gets a problem for balance)
-Dodge tanking is wayyyy too good. I'm glad it's not bad (too many games do that), but you can literally get to the point where you will never get hit. I have one character that I think could honestly solo every single mission at this point, it'd just be slow.
-And dodge tanking being broken is ever worse because regular tanks don't get similar mechanics. Block is awful because there's so few things to stack it. I can have 180 dodge, but I can only have like 50-60 block?
-Enemy encounters are generally simple and the same. Even worse lategame when you just wait for them to approach and do your best to oneshot the entire board before they can even counter. This becomes even worse because boss fights have to either have ludicrous HP values or crazy damage to make them even slightly challenging, which just feels unfun.
-A lack of lategame items for certain archetypes. A set of purple daggers I got have been the best I've seen so far and I'm on the last mission. Took me AGES to find an okay sword and shield.
-The list of things I've encountered which bug me goes on and on.

It's like everywhere I look, I hate the design choices or lack thereof, but the gameplay loop is still fun, and the story (although cliche) is interesting enough. I think with another year of dev time, this game could've turned from a game I was constantly asking "what? why?" about to one of my favorite games this year.

On paper I like a lot of the concepts of this game.

I keep finding myself looking to try games similar to those that I enjoy.

Our Adventurer Guild wants to bridge the gap between something like Fire Emblem and a management game. Conceptually this sounds very cool. Where it falls apart is it only functions as these things at a very basic level. It's a very basic strategy rpg and it's a very basic management sim.

Not the end of the world for the price if you're looking for something to play and this games genres speak to you. I think more on a is it worth my time basis these days as someone getting older and having less time to play games. Games that want to waste my time for tedious progression or retreading the same ground are getting much less fun for me as I get older.

I don't think this game is horrible, but I'm not wanting to turn it on much anymore. I can see the scope of the game as I start to dig in here and realize there is going to be a lot of time sink. I checked some videos and read some more on the game to see that there will definitely be quite a lot of time sink. When I say time sink, I specifically mean moments where I'm just grinding basic non-engaging elements to reach the next story beat or progress in the game.

Some people will love this, I am not one of those people. I may give it another shot down the road, but today this ain't it.

I was really torn on writing this review on do i recommend it or not...

I would say, its still a no recommendation from me, the game has a lot of flaws and things to improve. However, it is still enjoyable for first 20 or so hours, So if you are ok with that, then maybe its worth a buy.

Ultimately i would say its 51 no and 49 yes from me.

First of, do not expect a survival game, this is more of a sandbox, choose your own adventure type of a game. You can take all the time you want (mostly, there is a wage system in the game, but its really lackluster and easy, time passage means very little), you can just use one A-team of 6 adventurers that are strongest (story ones included who are usually stronger than the generic ones), you can just grind out levels until you are ready to tackle on next big quest.

As for the combat system itself, it is...good at first, but later on turns into just one- shooting everything, and also being one-shooted, especially on hardest difficulty, the combat is SUPER fast, and everything is skewed towards gaming the system and using broken OP classes such as bard (more on this later).... HOWEVER, nothing comes close to the ridiculous system that this game has and imposes upon you... The Bravery points:

What bravery points are, is a way for your adventurer to act multiple times in one turn... So what that means is... That not only is everything dying in just few hits, but you can just use bravery points to use ur adventurer to do this MULTIPLE times in one turn?! Like what did they activate time stop ability?! Its ridiculously bad and gamey design, that unfortunately past early game is mandatory to be used, as enemies scale in numbers forcing you to use this system even if you dont want it.

When you go on a quest, you are presented with a local map, in which you can find enemy groups to fight, and here is where mid-to-late game completely falls apart, the idea is, that you fight these groups, you lose hp and sp, and you need to rest and camp, meaning that there is some challenge involved, right? Wrong. Since you have Bard, now you can just recover all of your sp, hp and BP so no camping needed.

Overall, its a game that needs a lot of work and improvements to make it balanced and turn it into a game, the foundation is good, the idea is good, I hope dev improves the game with DLC and considers changing the combat system.

Thank you for reading.

"Cute" mission based adventurer guild management game with tactical turn-based combat, but... grindy-ness, limitations, shallow combat, and no incentive for multiple groups makes it a letdown. 4.5/10

3 main issues ruined the game for me and left a bad taste after completion:
1 - If you don't play the way the creator wants you to with the exact class setup they want you to, you're punished significantly over time. You can't have multiple high level class characters, only one of each. You can't have multiple characters with the same title. You can't find/make most gear to support multiple characters of the same class, etc. Some builds never even get a good weapon option. The game straightjackets you for no reason.

And 2 - Even though my characters have dozens of abilities to use in combat by the end of the game, only 1-3 per character are ever worth using in any situation. So don't make my mistake: combat does *not* become more interesting as you go. It's almost exactly the same as level 1 but with bigger numbers. In other words: boring.

3 - Endgame is a disaster. Ultimate skills consume huge amounts of BP, which makes no sense and usually makes them worse than just getting extra actions to spam low level abilities. Enemies all too easy or too hard. Enemy resist penetration and immunities gimp what you've been doing for 30+ hours. You can't just redo your titles at that point without a huge time investment, so you can be screwed. It's all just not fun in the slightest and ends up with you doing chip damage with combo attacks over and over and over...


Pros:
- Good number of game options like permadeath, etc
- Main gameplay of "hopping" around collecting resources and choosing/avoiding fights is fine
- Boss fights are fun (until late game)
- You can create adventurers or use story characters, or both

Cons:
- Combat too simple. Not enough abilities, synergies, classes or strategies. What does exist is unoriginal and has been done a million times before in a million other games
- Some abilities and mechanics are blatantly stolen from other games, verbatim
- Too easy overall
- A couple massive difficulty spikes that take away from the experience
- Not enough options to avoid time-wasting trash fights
- Too grindy. You need to do entire quests just to get 1 part of 1 piece of gear... then do it *again* to upgrade it. Ugh
- Tries too hard to be "cute" with dialogue
- *MASSIVE* amounts of dialogue that says nothing. Literally thousands and thousands of lines of fluff
- Sometimes game gets stuck in uninterruptible infinite loop of dialogue
- Some quests have enemies that heal from taking a type of damage, with no warning before going on the mission. If you take a specialist mage of that element along, you are severely gimped
- No reason to have multiple adventurer groups, completely invalidating half the game
- Killing bosses with multiple forms gives them free attacks in the new form immediately, easily resulting in character deaths even though you were supposed to go next in the turn order

TL;DR: Stay away from this derivative, unbalanced garbage.

A nice lite tactical game. It's just passable but still a negative. It has tons of goods, but also equally as much bads and some shady tricks that ultimately made me gave it a negative. Overall, for it's price, it's good, especially for a tactical game. Should keep you entertained for awhile.

Pros:
1. Nice variety of classes and almost all can be overpower if built and equipped correctly. Especially so if you decided to build a sole single class party.

2. Being able to adjust resources and experience. You can grind if you want. But if you don't then adjust settings to your liking to help ease the grind. This is always a BIG plus in games.

3. Many ways to win battles. Some gimmicks are more superior than others. These tactics works even in hard mode with ironman and even against bosses. Up to you to decide if it's you being a tactical genius or just cheating.

4. A nice system involving a bravery mechanic. Unfortunately, the whole battle system evolves around this. Can be easily abused after some experience and only detrimental in the beginning.

5. Simple managing system. Both at hub and during quests. Could use more in depth system though, but it does the job.


Cons:
1. Very inaccurate accuracy issue. Display 95% but you miss 1/4 of the time. Don't mind missing, but I do mind if game lies about it. Enemies also consistently hit you below 50%, even at 5%, but they can be adjusted. The whole thing could be a flaw in the system, since dev did stat using only Unity default RND, but dev probably be lying because there are other shady things I've noticed.

2. Enemies can and will crit you with a 0% chance. They will also usually take minimum damage from your damage range. And, In mid+ game, enemies have the ability to reduced your damage even more via glancing blows. They will abuse it, if you will deal just enough damage to kill them.

3. The traits on the adventurers are imbalance. The bad traits adventurer are completely useless around mid game, and they show up a lot on the random recruits until end game.

4. 90% of battle maps start you off at a disadvantage. Height advantages means nothing because most maps just abuse it to hinder you. Height differences also effects your adventurers a lot but is moot to enemies. There are also objects, but most of them are placed somewhere in outer space. Unless, it's a bad object. Then it's placed right in your face. This just leads you to abuse AI instead of playing tactically.

5. In mid+ games, each battles have a ton of enemies. So much so it becomes atrocious. A cheap way to boost difficulty but makes the game extremely long and boring. Once again leads to abusing AI and cheap mechanics.

I want to like this game, but I don't. I've bounced off a few times.

In the early game, there's not much there in terms of tactics. Battle areas are small and feel cramped. Your mercenaries start with identical equipment; first they have Bad Sword, and eventually upgrade to Bad Sword +1 after you collect enough iron scraps on the adventure maps. Monsters can usually move further than you can, and usually have more range, so there's definitely a feeling of needing to take a few hits in order to end things decisively rather than trying to win through conservative play. It's also very much a turn-based kiting game; creatures can freely engage and disengage (i.e. run past your fighters to kill your mage), so you end up acclimating to this weird pattern of movement where characters are just running around on the basis of who can cast what at what range - there's not much sense of formation.

As I said, I've bounced off this a few times. Combat aside, management feels like an exercise in irritation. I don't like how much money it costs and how many steps are involved in healing my characters and keeping their moods high. I don't like how incredibly slow the natural healing process is, and I don't like that adventurers take a mood hit when they get magically healed - feels like a very weird design decision. Overall, management of the team feels boring and slow - I'm constantly looking at a roster of adventurers that can't do anything because they're either chopping wood, drunk, sad, and hurt.

Therefore, I hire more mercs: now I don't like having to decide whether I should hire the mage with a penalty to wisdom or the fighter who is cowardly and has a penalty to endurance (because that's all that the RNG decided to give me this time.)

So yeah, the gameplay loop is something of a chore for me, but the icing on the cake is the dialog, which is competently written but painfully childish. It feels like bring trapped in a D-list shonen anime. "WaaaaAAAAHHH! Oh no, she's scary when she's angry!" "Why you, I'll show you scary!!!" There are like 15 NPCs in your camp who are all these ridiculous anime caricatures and I effing hate it.

The anime art style itself is not bad, but it's also not good, and it's always sort of in the middle of the screen, like a teenager's art book that you can't look away from.

Overall, this feels like the sort of thing I would have found on Newgrounds ages ago. It's not junk - it really isn't - but I think there's a reason that this game is a niche success rather than a minor mainstream success. And it's not for me.
